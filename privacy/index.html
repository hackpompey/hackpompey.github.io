Who we are

Hack Pompey LTD looks after your information

Hack Pompey is run by Hack Pompey LTD, a company registered in England and Wales with the number 11486057. We decide what happens to the information on this page. In data protection law that makes us the controller. The law in question is the UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018.

This page covers this website and everything else.

This website

This website collects nothing

hackpompey.co.uk is a set of plain pages. There are no forms that send anything to us, no cookies, no analytics and no tracking scripts. We cannot see who visits.

GitHub hosts the pages, and every visit passes through Cloudflare, which runs our domain name. To get a page to you, they see your IP address and the details every browser sends when it asks for a page. Any website host does the same. They handle it under their own privacy policies, which are linked further down.

Some links take you to other sites, such as Eventbrite, a Google Form or our Discord. Once you are there, that site's own privacy policy applies.

What we have

What we have, and why

Everything here came to us from somewhere other than this website. For each one we say what it is, why we have it, and the reason the law lets us use it.

Two of those reasons need explaining. Some things we need to run an event you signed up for. Others we do because of our legitimate interests. That means we have a fair reason of our own, and it does not unfairly affect you. You can object to anything we do for that reason, and we will stop unless we have a strong reason to carry on.

How long we keep it. We keep what we collect for five years, then delete it. It either helps us run the event it came from or helps us plan the next ones. You can ask us to delete yours sooner, at any time. Our Discord, photos and past event pages work differently, and their sections below say how.

Tickets for in-person events

Our events in Portsmouth use Eventbrite for tickets. When you book, Eventbrite collects your name, your email address and your answers to the booking questions, and passes them to us.

Some of those questions are there so we can run a good event, such as asking about your dietary needs. We use those answers, with your name and email address, to run the event you booked. That means knowing who is coming, letting you in, feeding you and contacting you about the day. The law allows it because you signed up for the event and we need these details to run it.

Other questions help us with our goals as a company, which are about tech for good, such as reaching the right people for future events. The reason for those is legitimate interests.

We keep ticket details for five years, like everything else. Eventbrite keeps its own copy for as long as its privacy policy says.

Project submissions

At our events we collect what teams submit about their projects. This can include your name, your email address, your social media handles and whatever the project needs us to know. Past events used a Google Form for this.

We use it to run the event, such as judging and presenting the projects, and in what we send and publish after the event, such as the write-up on this website. The reason is the same as for tickets. You entered, and we need this to run the event.

Tools we build for our events

Some events use tools that Hack Pompey and its organisers built themselves. People's Choice voting is one. The voting app held only the project names, which we typed in, and the votes. Each device got its own random ID, made in the browser, so it could vote once. The app did not collect names, email addresses or IP addresses.

Our Discord server

The Hack Pompey Discord is where we talk to people during and between events. Discord holds your account, your profile and what you post. As organisers we can see what you post in our server, and we can remove messages and members.

We use it to answer questions, share news and keep the server safe. The reason is legitimate interests. We run a community for the people who come to our events, and a server needs moderating.

Your messages stay until you delete them, we remove them or Discord does. You can leave the server at any time.

Emails

If you email contact@hackpompey.co.uk or partners@hackpompey.co.uk, we have your email address and whatever you write. We use it to reply and to deal with what you asked. The reason is legitimate interests. You wrote to us, and we need your message to answer it.

Google Workspace hosts our email. Hack Pompey's organisers read it. We keep emails for five years.

Event emails

We send news about our events, and promotional emails, through Eventbrite. Promotional emails are opt-in: you only get them if you said yes when you booked. Every email has an unsubscribe link, or you can email us to be taken off.

Photos at in-person events

We take photos at our events in Portsmouth. Some go on this website's past event pages and some on our Instagram and Facebook. More go in shared Google Photos and Google Drive albums that those pages link to.

We do this to show what the events are like, so more people want to come. The reason is legitimate interests.

You can ask not to be photographed. When you arrive, you pick a wristband or lanyard that tells our photographers whether you are happy to be in photos. You can also tell any organiser on the day. We do our best, but sometimes somebody still ends up in a photo.

You can ask us to take a photo down. If a photo of you is published and you want it gone, email contact@hackpompey.co.uk and we will remove it. You do not need to give a reason. If you are under 18, a parent or carer can ask for you.

Names and links on past event pages

Our write-ups of past events name the winners, the teams and the people who helped. Some link to their GitHub, their LinkedIn or their projects. The reason is legitimate interests. We keep a record of each event and give people credit for what they built.

If you want your name or a link added, changed or removed, email us and we will update the page. Where we link to your profile because you asked us to, you can change your mind at any time.

Other companies

Who else handles it

These companies hold or carry some of the information above for us. Each one handles it under its own privacy policy.

  • Eventbrite runs our ticket sales and sends our event emails. Eventbrite's privacy policy
  • Google hosts our email, and runs the submission form and the photo albums. Google's privacy policy
  • Discord runs our server. Discord's privacy policy
  • Meta runs Instagram and Facebook, where we post photos. Meta's privacy policy
  • GitHub hosts this website. GitHub's privacy statement
  • Cloudflare runs our domain name and hosts the tools we build for events. Cloudflare's privacy policy

Some of these companies are based in the United States and keep data there. Their policies say how they protect it once it leaves the UK.

Young people

If you are under 18

Our in-person events welcome people under 18. Anybody under 16 must have a parent or carer with them for the whole event. If you are 16 or 17, you can come on your own and do not need anybody's permission.

Hack Pompey Online is for people aged 13 and over. It runs on Discord, and Discord accounts are for people aged 13 and over, so nobody younger can take part.

Everything on this page applies to you whatever your age. You can ask us for any of the things below yourself, and a parent or carer can ask for you.

Your rights

What you can ask us to do

  • Send you a copy of the information we have about you.
  • Correct anything that is wrong.
  • Delete your information.
  • Stop using it, for example by taking down a photo.
  • Stop doing something you said yes to, such as linking to your profile.

Email contact@hackpompey.co.uk. It is free. We will reply within a month. We may need to check it is really you first. If we cannot do what you ask, we will tell you why.

Complaints

If you are not happy

Tell us first at contact@hackpompey.co.uk and we will try to put it right.

You can also complain to the Information Commissioner's Office, the UK's data protection regulator. You do not have to come to us first. Make a complaint to the ICO.
